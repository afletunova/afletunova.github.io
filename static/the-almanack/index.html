Anthropic's Claude Cowork: Revolutionising AI with Built-in Browsing Capabilities

Anthropic has unveiled a significant upgrade to its Claude Cowork application by integrating a dedicated browser directly into the desktop app. This advancement marks a substantial step in enhancing AI-driven task automation across varied digital environments.

The Discovery/Development

Anthropic, a leading innovator in artificial intelligence, has developed an in-app browser within its Claude Cowork software. Announced on August 27, 2026, by Maximilian Schreiner, this feature allows Claude to autonomously navigate, read, click, and type on websites, enabling it to complete tasks such as form-filling and data extraction from dashboards in real time. This in-app browser is distinct from user’s personal browsers, maintaining privacy by keeping tabs, bookmarks, and passwords separate. The feature is currently available for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise customers.

Why This Matters

This development addresses a crucial limitation in previous AI applications — their reliance on APIs to interact with websites. By embedding a browser, Claude can now access information and perform functions on sites without dedicated APIs, significantly broadening its operational scope. This capability empowers businesses and individual users who rely on dynamic content and interactive web features, facilitating seamless automation and efficiency in digital tasks.

How It Works

The browser resides within Claude Cowork, opening in a side panel when a task requires web interaction. Claude can perform a range of actions autonomously, such as filling forms or extracting data, without needing direct human intervention. Data privacy is maintained as the application's internal browser remains separate from personal browsing data. Users can import specific logins if necessary, although sensitive sites like banking and email remain out of bounds.

Implications and Future Directions

The integration of a browser sets a new benchmark for AI software capabilities, enhancing the functionality of AI-driven workplace tools. As automation becomes more embedded in business processes, Claude's abilities present new opportunities for innovation and efficiency. Looking ahead, this could lead to broader AI applicability in areas lacking API access. However, prompt injection risks underline the need for ongoing vigilance and adherence to trusted sites.

Expert Perspectives

As Anthropic continues developing Claude, addressing these vulnerabilities will be a critical task.

Uncertain Future: U.S. Federal Cuts Threaten Cognitive Science Advancement

The recent exclusion of the Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences Directorate from the National Science Foundation’s funding spotlight reveals a precarious future for key research areas. Experts fear detrimental impacts on cognitive neuroscience and related fields.

The Discovery/Development

The National Science Foundation (NSF), a major benefactor of cognitive and behavioural research in the U.S., omitted the Directorate for Social, Behavioral, and Economic Sciences (SBE) from its latest funding opportunities. Typically responsible for over 60% of federal funding in these areas, its absence raises significant concern about continuity and future research dynamics. This development follows President Trump's budget request, which proposed dramatic cuts to the NSF, eliminating specific funding for the SBE.

Why This Matters

This funding gap jeopardises invaluable sectors such as anthropology, developmental science, and linguistics—all critical for understanding various cognitive processes. The SBE's continued operation is uncertain, threatening the support framework for research that often answers foundational questions about human behaviour and societal interactions. The broader implications touch numerous scientific and policy-related areas, impairing advancements that inform public health, education, and economic strategies.

How It Works

Typically, NSF allocates funds based on comprehensive planning and consultation with scientific communities, allowing flexibility to adapt to emerging priorities. However, the current situation, influenced by political directives, bypasses traditional expert consultations—potentially undermining the historically autonomous decision-making process within the NSF. The result is a fragmented approach that could push researchers to seek alternative funding sources, often less suitable for foundational research objectives.

Implications and Future Directions

Without corrective measures, this shift may stymie progress in cognitive neuroscience, a field heavily reliant on SBE funding to explore basic brain functionalities. Many researchers, such as Rhea Eskew from Northeastern University, have already downsized their operations. While some programs, like the Collaborative Research in Computational Neuroscience, might find footing under other criteria aligned with administrative priorities, the loss of a dedicated directorate places undue strain on maintaining intellectual continuity.

Expert Perspectives

Wendy Naus, executive director of the Consortium of Social Science Associations, emphasises the existential threat posed by dismantling the SBE. The challenge lies in maintaining the infrastructure to quickly recover funding streams when political tides shift. Similarly, Sepideh Sadaghiani from the University of Illinois notes the vital role of SBE in funding primary research essential for clinical applications. The potential misalignment in reviewer expertise once the SBE is fragmented could lead to further inefficiencies.

The Bigger Picture

This scenario underscores deeper challenges within the intersection of neuroscience and policy-making. As researchers grapple with these realities, broader questions emerge about the role of political influence on scientific discovery processes. Ensuring a sustainable pathway for behavioural sciences amidst fiscal uncertainties is crucial for maintaining the field's momentum and impact. The implications for AI and neuroscience safety are profound, as foundational research often provides the underpinnings for technological and ethical advancements.
