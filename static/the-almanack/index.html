The Discovery/Development

In a remarkable study led by Ryohei Watanabe and colleagues, it was discovered that polyubiquitinated tau filaments from Alzheimer’s disease and vacuolar tauopathy exhibit distinct structural differences when influenced by polyubiquitin. The research, published in Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, employs cryo-electron microscopy to highlight these changes. By chemically modifying polyubiquitin with the small molecule ubistatin B, the team was able to reposition poorly structured densities toward positively charged residues on the tau core filament, consequently altering the filament’s interface.

Why This Matters

Tauopathies, including Alzheimer’s disease, involve the accumulation of tau filaments that are central to neurodegenerative processes. Understanding the stabilisation and propagation of these filaments is crucial, as these mechanisms contribute to disease progression. This study provides a fresh perspective on how post-translational modifications (PTMs) can influence tau filament structures, possibly opening routes to preventing or reversing filament aggregation.

How It Works

The research utilised cryo-EM to examine the differences in tau filament structures. By applying ubistatin B, a chemical change in the polarity of polyubiquitin was induced. This repositioning led to a noticeable shift in the protofilament–protofilament interface among tau filaments from vacuolar tauopathy. Such structural alterations suggest that PTMs, like ubiquitination, play a role in modulating the stability and propagation of tau filaments, offering a potential target for therapeutic intervention.

Implications and Future Directions

This study sets a new precedent in tauopathy research by demonstrating the potential of chemical modulation in restructuring tau filaments.
